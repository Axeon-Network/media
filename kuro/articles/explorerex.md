***Turning Windows 11 Into Windows XP, Kinda... (ExplorerEx)*** is an [AstroNT](AstroNT) video made by [AveryEclipse](AveryEclipse). Released on 28 February 2026, the video revolves around ExplorerEx, which takes the taskbar and start menu from Windows XP/Server 2003 and makes it its own thing, which can be used even in modern versions of Windows such as Windows 11, a VM of which was used for the demonstration in the video.

{% include items/embed.html image_src='astront/misc/wave1.png' caption='Original planning of "Wave 1". 17 September 2025.' %}

In the original plannings, the video was supposed to be done by [KitSixtyFour](KitSixtyFour) and released by 2 December 2025, being the last in the list of videos originally planned for the initial wave of videos (colloquially and retroactively referred to as "Wave 1"). This never happened, however, as there haven't been actual uploads on the channel for two months after the release of *[Discord on Windows Vista but EVERYTHING Goes Wrong](Discord_on_Windows_Vista_but_EVERYTHING_Goes_Wrong)*, with the next video planned to follow up "Replacing ProgMan" being merged into the planned *[X Minutes of Useless Windows Information](X_Minutes_of_Useless_Windows_Information)* project, and *[Running Linux in... Year 10000?](Running_Linux_in_Year_10000?)*, also made by Avery, having been edited on-and-off although already being recorded prior to Discord on Vista's release.

The idea wouldn't be picked up again until after the release of *[The Return of the Windows Store in Windows 8.1?! (8Store)](The_Return_of_the_Windows_Store_in_Windows_8.1?!_(8Store))*. After it began blowing up in views on 22 February 2026, Avery, with the experience previously accumulated from previous channels like [Lynxmic](Lynxmic), was looking for a good idea for a video to grow alongside the 8Store video. At the end it was decided to finally take the ExplorerEx video out the shelf, this time made by Avery.

Production of the video was done within the span of a few days, with the recordings being made on 24 February 2026 and editing done by 28 February 2026, when the video was uploaded to YouTube. As of 16 September 2026, the video has 5,373 views, by far the fastest growing "Wave 1" video in the first 24 hours.

In an [AveryWebsite](AveryWebsite) [blog post](https://avery.is-a.dev/blog/2026-03-05-growing-youtube-channel) published on 5 March 2026, Avery revealed that there has been a major recording incident where cursor capturing in OBS was unintentionally left disabled throughout the recordings, something they didn't notice until after they were done recording. As such, the video had to be re-recorded from scratch. The original recordings were since deleted.
